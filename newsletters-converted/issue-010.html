Building a Competitive Intelligence Engine at SAP Concur

Lauren Cochrane is leading the evaluation and rollout of two vendors at SAP Concur: Crayon for competitive intelligence and Clozd for win-loss analysis. The goal? A centralized competitive intelligence engine that serves sales, marketing, and product teams.

Our Google Antigravity Beta Test

SAP is one of the early enterprise testers of Google Antigravity — Google's new "agent-first" IDE that launched earlier this year. Unlike traditional coding assistants that autocomplete lines, Antigravity deploys autonomous agents that can plan, code, execute commands, and even browse the web to help you build.

Think of it as a "Mission Control" for AI-powered development. It's powered by Gemini 3 and has hit 6% developer adoption in just two months — one of the fastest-growing AI dev tools on the market.

I'm our team's representative on the SAP beta evaluation, and this week we hit our first real friction point.

🔧 What is Google Antigravity?

An "agent-first" IDE that treats AI not as a sidebar assistant, but as an autonomous actor capable of planning, executing, and verifying complex engineering tasks. It can run commands, browse documentation, and chain multi-step workflows with minimal human intervention.

The Issue: Sandbox Mode Kills Network Access

When running Antigravity on Windows (via WSL2), the tool's "strict mode" — which Google recommends for enterprise security — enforces a sandbox that blocks all network access. Not just corporate network. All network. You can't even run pip install.

The problem: Basic prompts work fine. But the moment Antigravity tries to execute actual code or commands, it fails. For a tool designed to autonomously build and deploy, this is a dealbreaker.

The Kicker: Mac Users Have Options, Windows Users Don't

Mac users see settings to toggle "allowed network for sandbox." Windows users don't see those options at all. Google is investigating whether this is a Windows-specific limitation or a configuration gap.

What This Means

Enterprise AI tooling is moving fast, but the security/usability tradeoff is real. Strict sandboxing makes sense for compliance — but if it makes the tool unusable, what's the point?

We're working with Google to find a middle ground that works for SAP's security posture without neutering the core functionality.

The SaaS Reckoning Is Here

Salesforce is down 37% YTD. Snowflake is down 44%. Adobe is down 35%. The narrative? AI copilots and agents are "compressing growth and pricing power" across CRM and adjacent categories.

Meanwhile, HubSpot just did something interesting: they shifted to outcome-based pricing. Their AI agents now cost $0.50 per resolved conversation (was $1/conversation). Pay for results, not usage.

Why This Matters for Us

This isn't just a pricing story. It's a positioning story, a commercial strategy story, and an enablement story — all wrapped into one. It maps directly to the skills we need to develop:

  • Understanding the shift: The market is forcing a reckoning on value articulation
  • Commercial strategy: Pricing models shifting from seats → usage → outcomes
  • AI-ready positioning: Must survive summarization AND justify premium
  • Champion enablement: ROI proof, not feature lists
