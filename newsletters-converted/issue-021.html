📖 This Week's Read

PMM Is Forking Into Two Different Jobs

Last week at Cannes, Forrester dropped a finding that should have gotten more attention: nine in ten agencies now use AI, but the way they're using it is undermining marketing effectiveness. The efficiency-creativity tradeoff is real—and it's reshaping what PMM means.

I wrote a piece this week synthesizing the evidence: the Forrester data, Adobe's agentic partnerships, Amazon Rufus conversion rates, and the Klaviyo trust research. The pattern that emerges is a bifurcation—PMM splitting into two distinct paths:

🔧 AI System Architects

Train models on positioning. Design agentic workflows. Build the orchestration layer that makes AI marketing actually work.

✨ Human Premium Specialists

Strategic judgment. Customer empathy from lived experience. Narrative craft. The things AI can't replicate.

The middle ground—competent execution of standard PMM tasks—is exactly where AI is most disruptive. The article maps five proof points from the past month and connects them back to what we're teaching in the curriculum.

🎯 Coming Up: Unit 5

Sales Enablement and the Death of the Static Battlecard

Purchase Stage · 45 minutes

It's 4:47 PM on a Tuesday. A Slack message lands: "I'm going up against [Competitor] tomorrow morning. Our battlecard is from Q3. Anything new?"

Unit 5 tackles why the static battlecard is already dead—not because battlecards aren't useful, but because the format assumes competitive intelligence is stable enough to be captured in a document and refreshed quarterly. That world is gone.

You'll learn:

  • The Enablement Paradox: Sales reps now have their own AI tools—what do they still need PMM for?
  • How to shift from artifact factory to intelligence layer
  • Building RAG-powered knowledge bases as competitive moats
  • Why the quality of your internal knowledge base is about to become one of your most important competitive advantages
