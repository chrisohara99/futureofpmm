## Opening Scenario: The Invisible Leader

Last November, one of our enterprise sales teams lost a $4.2M deal to a competitor we'd never considered a threat—a mid-sized startup with maybe 200 employees. In the post-mortem, the champion admitted something surprising: "My team's AI assistant kept surfacing them. Every time we asked about data integration solutions, they came up with strong positioning. You guys barely appeared."

We pulled the transcripts. The prospect's team had used Claude and Perplexity to research vendors. They'd asked questions like "best enterprise data fabric for multi-cloud environments" and "SAP vs Databricks for real-time analytics." In nine out of twelve queries, the startup appeared in the top recommendations. SAP appeared in four—and in two of those, the description was outdated.

We had better technology. Better references. Better pricing. But in the research layer—where shortlists get built—we were invisible.

This unit is about fixing that.

## The Discovery Shift

### How B2B Research Worked (2015-2023)

```
Buyer has problem
    ↓
Google search → clicks top 5 results
    ↓
Visits vendor websites
    ↓
Downloads gated content (enters lead funnel)
    ↓
Vendor nurtures → SDR outreach
    ↓
Discovery call
```

PMMs optimized for this flow. SEO mattered. Gated content captured leads. We controlled information access.

### How B2B Research Works Now

```
Buyer has problem
    ↓
Asks AI assistant → gets synthesized answer
    ↓
AI cites sources: G2, Gartner, docs, Reddit
    ↓
Buyer has shortlist before visiting any website
    ↓
Visits 2-3 vendor sites for validation only
    ↓
First human contact: buyer already informed
```

The middle of the funnel collapsed. Buyers don't "discover" vendors—AI assistants curate them.

## The Visibility Stack

After analyzing 200+ AI queries across our product portfolio, we identified four layers that determine whether AI assistants surface and accurately represent SAP solutions.

### Layer 1: Structured Documentation
**Question:** Can AI parse and quote your content?

AI assistants extract information more reliably from:
- Clean HTML with semantic markup (not PDFs)
- Clear headings that match common queries
- Explicit capability statements ("SAP Datasphere does X, Y, Z")
- Structured comparison tables
- FAQ sections with specific questions

**Common SAP failure modes:**
- Product information locked in PDFs
- Marketing fluff without concrete capabilities
- Jargon that doesn't match buyer terminology
- Outdated documentation that contradicts current messaging

### Layer 2: Third-Party Validation
**Question:** Do credible external sources confirm your positioning?

AI assistants heavily weight:
- G2 and Gartner Peer Insights reviews
- Analyst reports (Gartner MQ, Forrester Wave)
- Technical comparison sites
- Industry publications
- Reddit and community discussions

**The validation gap:** We often have strong analyst positions but weak G2 presence. Or strong customer satisfaction but few public reviews. AI sees the gaps.

### Layer 3: Competitive Context
**Question:** Does AI understand your differentiation?

AI assistants build mental models of competitive landscapes. They need:
- Clear "us vs. them" content (done tastefully)
- Explicit differentiation statements
- Use-case-specific positioning
- Acknowledgment of where competitors are strong (builds credibility)

**The nuance trap:** PMMs often write positioning that's clear to humans who understand context but opaque to AI that's trying to build a category map.

### Layer 4: Consistency
**Question:** Does your story hold together across sources?

AI assistants synthesize across sources. They notice:
- Contradictions between website and documentation
- Positioning that doesn't match analyst descriptions
- Customer reviews that conflict with marketing claims
- Outdated content that's still indexed

**The consistency tax:** Every inconsistency creates doubt. AI assistants may hedge recommendations or surface the inconsistency directly to users.

## The GEO Framework

Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) isn't SEO. It's a different discipline.

| SEO (Search Engine Optimization) | GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) |
|----------------------------------|--------------------------------------|
| Optimize for ranking position | Optimize for accurate citation |
| Keywords and backlinks | Clear statements and structure |
| Drive clicks to your site | Get included in synthesized answers |
| Compete for attention | Compete for recommendation |
| User visits your content | AI summarizes your content |

### GEO Principles for SAP Content

**1. Lead with the answer**
Don't bury capabilities in marketing narrative. Start with explicit statements that AI can extract.

❌ "In today's complex data landscape, organizations need unified approaches..."
✅ "SAP Datasphere connects and integrates data across hybrid multi-cloud environments, enabling real-time analytics without data movement."

**2. Use the buyer's language**
Match terminology to how buyers actually ask questions—not internal product names.

❌ "BTP Intelligent Suite capabilities"
✅ "AI-powered automation for SAP S/4HANA processes"

**3. Make comparisons explicit**
AI assistants are constantly building competitive maps. Help them.

❌ Avoiding competitor names entirely
✅ "Unlike traditional data warehouses, SAP Datasphere provides live data federation without requiring ETL pipelines."

**4. Structure for extraction**
Use formatting that AI can parse: headers, tables, numbered lists, FAQ sections.

**5. Keep current**
Outdated content creates inconsistency. Regular review cycles matter more than ever.

## The Monitoring System

Visibility isn't a one-time fix. AI training data updates, competitors publish new content, analyst opinions shift. You need ongoing monitoring.

### What to Track

**Monthly queries:** Run your Visibility Audit (Exercise 1) on the same prompts monthly. Track changes.

**Competitive movement:** Monitor when competitors appear/disappear from AI recommendations. What changed?

**Source citations:** Track which sources AI assistants cite for your category. Are you present in those sources?

**Accuracy drift:** Track when AI descriptions become outdated or inaccurate. Flag for content refresh.

### Team Ritual

Consider a monthly "AI Visibility Review" where the PMM team:
1. Runs standard queries against current AI assistants
2. Compares to previous month
3. Identifies top 3 visibility issues
4. Assigns ownership for fixes
5. Reviews fixes from previous month

## Key Principles (Summary)

1. **The research layer shifted.** AI assistants build shortlists before buyers visit websites. If you're not visible there, you're not in the consideration set.

2. **Four layers determine visibility.** Documentation structure, third-party validation, competitive context, and consistency across sources.

3. **GEO ≠ SEO.** Optimize for accurate citation in synthesized answers, not search ranking.

4. **Lead with answers.** AI extracts explicit statements. Marketing narrative gets lost.

5. **Monitor continuously.** Visibility changes as AI training data, competitors, and analyst opinions evolve.
