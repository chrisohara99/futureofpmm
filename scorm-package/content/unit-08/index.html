The Pricing Tectonic Shift

For decades, enterprise software pricing followed a simple formula: count the humans, multiply by a price. Named users. Concurrent users. Seats. The model was predictable, easy to budget, and aligned with how companies thought about software adoption.

Then three forces converged to break it:

  1. Cloud infrastructure made compute and storage variable costs, not fixed assets
  2. API-first architectures meant software increasingly talked to other software, not humans
  3. AI agents began doing work that humans used to do—but don't need "seats"

💡 Why This Matters for PMM

Pricing isn't just a finance or product decision—it's a positioning decision. How you price signals what you value, who you're for, and how you expect customers to measure success. As PMMs, we own the narrative that makes pricing make sense.

📌 The CIO.com Warning (November 2025)

"Fueled by workforce reductions, which are in turn driven by AI, vendors are abandoning per-seat pricing and moving to charging by consumption and/or agent interactions."

This shift is forcing CIOs to completely rethink negotiation tactics. Fixed annual contracts are giving way to variable consumption commitments with usage floors and ceilings.

🟢 SAP Datasphere Pricing Model (Public)

SAP Datasphere uses Business Technology Platform Capacity Units rather than per-user licensing:

  • Flexible allocation: CUs can be applied to compute, storage, or data services
  • Hyperscaler agnostic: Same CU model works across AWS, Azure, and GCP
  • Workload-based: The number of CUs depends on your specific workload, not user count
  • Adaptable: Scale up or down as data volumes and processing needs change

Benefits of Consumption Pricing

✅ For Customers

  • Pay only for actual usage
  • Scale costs with business value
  • No shelfware waste
  • Lower barrier to start

✅ For Vendors

  • Revenue grows with adoption
  • Natural expansion motion
  • Incentive to drive usage
  • Usage data informs product

⚠️ Bill Shock: The Consumption Pricing Risk

A recent E3 Magazine analysis warned about "cloud bill shock"—when customers see unexpectedly high invoices due to usage spikes they didn't anticipate.

This is why SAP (and most enterprise vendors) offer committed capacity options: buy a block of CUs upfront at a discount, with flexibility to use them across services.

Outcome-Based Pricing: The Frontier

The next evolution: charge based on the results the software delivers, not the resources it consumes.

Early Examples

  • Zendesk (2024): First major SaaS vendor with outcome-based AI pricing—billing per "Automated Resolution"
  • Salesforce Agentforce: Per-conversation model charging for agent interactions, not seats
  • Sierra AI: "We're only paid when we drive real results"—pure outcome model

💡 The Measurement Challenge

Outcome-based pricing sounds ideal but is hard to implement. Real business outcomes have multiple inputs. If an AI tool contributes to an outcome but the customer's team or other tools also play a role, how do you attribute success? This complexity is why only ~17% of enterprise SaaS vendors have implemented true outcome-based pricing.

The PMM's Role in Pricing

In many organizations, PMM's role in pricing varies. At SAP, Solution Managers within the PMM organization work directly with Commercial Strategy (CS) teams and the Pricing Council to shape and set prices. This means PMM isn't just telling the value story—we're often at the table when pricing decisions are made.

🟢 Positioning the CU Model

Old narrative: "How many named users do you need for HANA?"

New narrative: "How much data do you want to activate, and how fast do you need answers?"

The shift from counting people to measuring workload is the foundation for outcome-based pricing evolution.

Key Takeaways

  1. Pricing is evolving from seats → consumption → outcomes, driven by cloud economics and AI
  2. SAP's Capacity Unit model is consumption-based and positions well for AI workloads
  3. Hybrid models dominate because pure consumption or outcome pricing is still unpredictable
  4. PMMs own the value narrative that makes pricing make sense to buyers
  5. The future is outcome-linked, but measurement and attribution challenges remain
