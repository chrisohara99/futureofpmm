### This Week: Unit 8 Unlocks — Pricing & Packaging

---

**The Lead**

Last month, a competitor dropped their per-seat pricing entirely. No more $150/user/month. Instead: usage-based, outcome-linked, pay-for-what-you-deploy.

The sales team panicked. "How do we compete against free trials that never expire?"

But here's what they missed: the competitor wasn't undercutting us. They were *repositioning the conversation*. They moved from "how many seats do you need?" to "what outcomes do you expect?" That's a fundamentally different sale—and one that AI is forcing across the industry.

AI doesn't just change *what* we sell. It changes *how we price what we sell*.

When your product can do 10x the work with the same headcount, per-seat pricing breaks down. When AI agents handle tasks that used to require human licenses, the value metric shifts. When customers can deploy your AI features incrementally, they expect pricing that scales the same way.

This week's unit unpacks the new rules: **consumption-based models, AI-tier packaging, outcome-linked pricing, and the death of the "enterprise unlimited" deal.**

### 📚 Unit 8 Preview: The Pricing Evolution Framework

The core model you'll explore this week:

| Era | Model | Value Metric |
|-----|-------|--------------|
| **Legacy** | Per-seat licensing | Headcount |
| **SaaS 1.0** | Tiered subscription | Feature access |
| **SaaS 2.0** | Usage-based | Consumption |
| **AI Era** | Outcome-linked | Business results |

**The shift:** We're moving from "pay for access" to "pay for outcomes." AI accelerates this because the *value delivered* no longer correlates with *users deployed*.

**Key questions Unit 8 addresses:**
- How do you price AI features vs. core product?
- When does usage-based make sense vs. subscription?
- What's the "AI tier" strategy and when should you use it?
- How do you handle customers who want to "try AI" without committing?
