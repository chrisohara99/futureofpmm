The GEO Playbook: How to Get Cited by AI

Market Insights

GEO — Generative Engine Optimization — isn't about gaming algorithms. It's about making your content so clear, authoritative, and well-structured that AI has no choice but to cite you.

When a buyer asks an AI assistant "What's the best data platform for enterprise analytics?", the answer draws from thousands of sources. Your goal: be one of them. Better yet, be the one that gets quoted.

This playbook covers the four pillars of GEO: Schema Markup, Entity Establishment, Quotable Claims, and Authoritative Signals.

1. Schema Markup: Speak the Machine's Language

Schema.org markup is structured data that tells AI exactly what your content means. It's the difference between hoping an AI understands your page and ensuring it does.

Priority Schemas for B2B

Organization: Company name, logo, description, founding date, employee count

Product: Name, description, features, pricing model, category

FAQPage: Question-answer pairs that AI can directly extract

HowTo: Step-by-step processes that become featured snippets

Example: Organization Schema

{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "Organization",
  "name": "SAP",
  "description": "Enterprise software company providing ERP, analytics, and business technology solutions to 400,000+ customers worldwide",
  "foundingDate": "1972",
  "numberOfEmployees": "107000",
  "url": "https://www.sap.com"
}

2. Entity Establishment: Exist in the Knowledge Graph

AI models don't just search — they reason about entities. An "entity" is a distinct thing the AI recognizes: a company, product, person, or concept. If you're not an established entity, you're invisible.

How to Establish Your Entity

Wikipedia: The gold standard. A Wikipedia page signals you're notable enough to exist.

Wikidata: The structured database behind Wikipedia. Create entries for your company and products.

Consistent naming: Use the exact same name everywhere. "SAP Datasphere" not "Datasphere by SAP" or "SAP's Datasphere product."

Cross-linking: Mention your entity on authoritative third-party sites — analyst reports, press releases, partner pages.

3. Quotable Claims: Write for Extraction

AI doesn't summarize — it extracts and synthesizes. Your content needs claims that work as standalone fragments.

The Quotability Test

For every key claim, ask:

  • Is it specific? Numbers beat adjectives. "150+ integrations" beats "extensive integrations."
  • Is it standalone? Does the sentence make sense without context?
  • Is it unique? Could your competitor say the same thing?
  • Is it verifiable? Can it be cross-referenced with other sources?
❌ Weak

"We're a leading platform with best-in-class capabilities and world-class support."

✓ Strong

"SAP Datasphere connects to 150+ enterprise data sources and processes 2 billion transactions daily."

❌ Weak

"Our customers love our innovative solution."

✓ Strong

"92% of SAP Datasphere customers report ROI within 6 months, according to a 2025 TechValidate survey."

4. Authoritative Signals: Build Citation Gravity

AI weights sources by authority. Being mentioned on high-credibility sites creates "citation gravity" — you become more likely to be cited because you've been cited before.

Authority-Building Tactics

Analyst coverage: Get mentioned in Gartner, Forrester, IDC reports

Press mentions: TechCrunch, VentureBeat, industry publications

Academic citations: Research papers, university case studies

Partner ecosystems: Integration directories, marketplace listings

Customer evidence: Named case studies on recognizable brands

The GEO Audit Checklist

Run this check on your key product pages:

  • Schema.org markup implemented and validated
  • Company/product exists as Wikidata entity
  • Wikipedia page exists (or notability criteria met)
  • Key claims are specific, numbered, and verifiable
  • First paragraph contains standalone quotable statement
  • Consistent entity naming across all pages
  • Recent analyst or press mentions (last 6 months)
  • FAQ schema on high-traffic pages

Measuring GEO Success

Unlike SEO rankings, GEO success is harder to track. But it's not invisible:

  • AI mention testing: Regularly ask ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Claude about your category. Screenshot and track responses.
  • Brand accuracy: When AI mentions you, is the description correct? Outdated info is worse than no mention.
  • Competitive share: In category queries, how often are you mentioned vs. competitors?
  • Citation quality: Are you being quoted, or just listed? Quotes signal deeper authority.
